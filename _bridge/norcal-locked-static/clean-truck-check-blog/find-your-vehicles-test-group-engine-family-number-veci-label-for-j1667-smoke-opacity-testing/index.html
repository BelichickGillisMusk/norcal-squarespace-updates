Find Your Truck’s Engine Family / Test Group Number: Engine Year, Not the Door

Published September 12, 2025 · Updated November 19, 2026 · NorCal CARB Mobile

Booking a Clean Truck Check? Call or text (916) 890-4427. OBD is $75 per truck, OVI is $199, and motorhomes are $99/$229. Fleet of 3 or more trucks? Call for fleet rates: (916) 890-4427.

Why we ask for this number

The engine family (on newer labels it’s often called the test group) is printed on your engine’s emission control label, or ECL. Some people call it the VECI label. That label tells us the engine model year, and under the Clean Truck Check the engine year decides which test your truck gets:

  • 2013 and newer diesel engines (and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines): OBD scan, $75 per truck.
  • 2012 and older diesel engines: OVI, $199. That’s a SAE J1667 smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions equipment.
  • 2017 and older alternative-fuel engines: visual inspection only.

Go by the engine, not the year on the door or the registration. CARB points out most engines are one model year older than the truck, so a 2013 truck can have a 2012 engine and need the OVI. Send us a photo of the label when you book and we’ll show up with the right setup for your truck.

Where to look

The ECL is a sticker or plate on the engine itself. CARB requires it to stay where the engine maker put it. Common spots:

  • On the valve cover or the side of the engine block
  • On the engine near the intake or the front cover
  • On rear-engine motorhomes, open the rear engine hatch and check the top and sides of the engine

Look for words like "Engine Family," "Test Group," "Engine Model Year" or "Displacement." Snap a clear photo of the whole label, wiping off dust or oil first so every character reads.

What if the label is missing or unreadable?

It happens on older trucks and repowered engines. CARB requires every heavy-duty vehicle to have the engine maker’s ECL attached and fully legible, so get it handled before test day:

  1. Contact your dealer or the engine manufacturer with your engine serial number. Ask for the engine family information and a replacement label.
  2. The replacement has to go where the original was and match the engine serial number.
  3. If the replacement is backordered, CARB has an engine information form the manufacturer can fill out. Keep it in the cab until the new label arrives. CARB’s page: Clean Truck Check: What to Do When an ECL is Missing/Illegible.

Can’t find it at all? Call (916) 890-4427 or use the booking form and we’ll help you figure out where to look.

Motorhome owners

California-registered diesel motorhomes over 14,000 lbs are in the Clean Truck Check. Non-commercial motorhomes test once a year, and they stay on that yearly schedule after October 2027 when OBD trucks move to four times a year. Motorhomes registered outside California aren’t covered. Motorhome tests are $99/$229 depending on which test the engine year calls for.

Quick checklist before we come out

  • Photo of the ECL (engine family / test group and engine year)
  • VIN, plus engine make and model if the label is hard to read
  • The truck or rig parked where we can reach the engine and the exhaust
  • Your deadline from CTC-VIS. A passing test can go in up to 90 days early. See when your test is due.

Your credentialed tester sends the passing result to CARB. You don’t upload anything. For how the smoke test works, see how the J1667 smoke test works, or OBD vs OVI.
