J1667 Smoke Testing Explained: Which Trucks Get Smoked, Which Get Scanned (2026)

Published September 10, 2025 · Updated November 19, 2026 · NorCal CARB Mobile

The short version

Under the Clean Truck Check, not every truck gets smoked.

  • 2013 and newer diesel engines (and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines) get an OBD scan. No smoke test.
  • 2012 and older diesel engines get a smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions equipment. That’s the OVI, and the smoke test is where J1667 comes in.
  • 2017 and older alternative-fuel engines get the visual inspection only. No smoke test.

The year that counts is the engine model year on the emission control label (ECL), not the year on the door. CARB notes most engines are one model year older than the truck, so a 2013 truck can have a 2012 engine. Check the label.

What J1667 actually is

SAE J1667 is the Society of Automotive Engineers’ snap acceleration smoke test procedure for heavy-duty diesels. The tester quickly snaps the engine from idle while a smoke meter on the exhaust reads opacity: how much light the smoke blocks. CARB requires Clean Truck Check smoke tests on non-OBD trucks to use a J1667-compatible smoke meter, run by a CARB credentialed tester.

We run a Wager 6500 smoke opacity meter. Wager lists it as SAE J1667 compliant, and Robert H. Wager Co. is on CARB’s list of smoke meter makers that filed declarations of J1667 compliance. (CARB says that list isn’t a certification or an endorsement, so we don’t call it one.)

The opacity limits CARB uses

Here are CARB’s allowable smoke opacity levels for trucks with on-road engines:

EngineOpacity limit
Equipped with a DPF / Level 3 VDECS5%
Retrofitted with a Level 2 VDECS20%
No DPF/VDECS, 1997-2006 engine20%
No DPF/VDECS, 1991-1996 engine30%
No DPF/VDECS, pre-1991 engine40%

Got an off-road engine in an on-road truck? It’s rare, but those trucks do the opacity and visual inspection no matter the engine year, because there’s no OBD requirement for off-road engines.

How often, and when

  • 2026: Almost every truck tests twice a year, on deadlines set by your DMV registration month (or the last digit of the VIN for out-of-state and exempt-plate trucks). Your CTC-VIS account shows the exact dates.
  • Starting October 2027: OBD trucks go to four times a year. Non-commercial motorhomes and agricultural vehicles stay at once a year.
  • Up to 90 days early: A passing test can be submitted up to 90 days before the deadline, which leaves time for repairs.
  • Annual fee: $32.13 per vehicle now, $33.12 for deadlines on or after January 1, 2027.

Your credentialed tester sends the result to CARB. You don’t upload anything.

If it fails

Fix what needs fixing, then retest and pass before your deadline. That’s what the 90-day window is for. And if CARB mails you a Notice to Submit to Testing after roadside screening, you have 30 calendar days to get a passing test in.

Before we show up

  • Have the truck where we can reach the exhaust and the engine label.
  • Make sure the ECL is on the engine and readable. If it’s missing or worn off, CARB says to get the engine family info and a replacement label from your dealer or engine maker. (Our guide: find your engine label.)
  • Tell us the engine year when you book, so we bring the right setup.
