OVI Smoke Opacity Test for Bay Area and Central Valley Trucks

Published December 24, 2025 · Updated November 19, 2026 · NorCal CARB Mobile

If your truck has a 2012-or-older diesel engine and runs anywhere from the Bay Area to the Central Valley, its Clean Truck Check is an OVI: an SAE J1667 smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions equipment. We do it at your yard for $199, and 2013-and-newer engines get a $75 OBD scan instead.

Call (916) 890-4427 to book. OBD is $75 per truck, OVI is $199, and motorhomes are $99/$229. Fleet of 3 or more trucks? Call for fleet rates: (916) 890-4427.

Who needs the OVI

Under CARB's Clean Truck Check (the Heavy-Duty Inspection and Maintenance program), the test depends on the engine model year on the emission control label, not the year on the door.

  • 2012 and older diesel engines: OVI ($199). Smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions control equipment.
  • 2013 and newer diesel engines, and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines: OBD test ($75 per truck). No smoke test. We scan the engine's OBD data.
  • 2017 and older alternative-fuel engines: visual inspection only, no smoke test.
  • Motorhomes ($99/$229): Call and we'll tell you which one fits.

What happens during the test

The smoke opacity part follows SAE J1667, the snap-acceleration smoke test procedure for heavy-duty vehicles. A smoke meter reads the exhaust during the test, and the result is compared to CARB's limit for that engine. Then we do the visual inspection of the emissions equipment. The credentialed tester sends the result to CARB. You don't upload anything.

For CARB's opacity limits by engine year, see our SAE J1667 smoke test explainer.

Testing at your yard, from the Bay to the Valley

Plenty of 2012-and-older trucks still work drayage around the Bay, ag hauling in the Valley and construction in between. For 2012-and-older trucks, we can do an East Bay opacity-van pass at the yard so the SAE J1667 OVI test happens where the truck already sits.

We come to your yard, so you don't burn $8 diesel.

If a truck doesn't pass

CARB says to make the needed repairs, then test again and pass before the deadline. You can submit a passing test up to 90 days before a deadline, so testing early leaves room for a repair and a retest before the date.

How often

Most trucks test twice a year, every six months. Non-commercial motorhomes and agricultural vehicles test once a year. Your exact dates are in CTC-VIS, CARB's Clean Truck Check reporting system. Each truck also owes the annual fee: $32.13 per vehicle now, $33.12 for deadlines on or after January 1, 2027.
