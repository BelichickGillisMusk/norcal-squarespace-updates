# Clean Truck Check Sacramento: Yard Testing for Construction Fleets

> **Post context:** Location: Sacramento, Rancho Cordova, Granite Bay, Lincoln · Industry: construction · Test: OBD or smoke opacity + visual (OVI) · Official CARB source: [Clean Truck Check testing requirements](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements)

A construction truck that's parked at a testing station isn't pouring concrete, hauling base rock or getting a crew to the jobsite. That's the whole problem with Clean Truck Check for Sacramento builders. The test is quick. Getting the truck to the test isn't.

NorCal CARB Mobile fixes that by coming to you. We test at your yard or staging lot in Sacramento, Rancho Cordova, Granite Bay, Lincoln and the surrounding area, usually before the trucks roll out for the day.

## Which construction trucks need Clean Truck Check

Clean Truck Check covers [heavy-duty vehicles over 14,000 lbs GVWR](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-requirements-vehicles-subject-semi-annual-compliance) that operate in California. That includes privately owned and government fleets, and out-of-state trucks too. On a construction yard, that usually means:

- Concrete mixers and pump trucks
- Dump trucks and end dumps
- Water trucks and tractors pulling equipment trailers
- Larger service-body and flatbed trucks, if their GVWR is over 14,000 lbs

That last group is where people get caught. A medium-duty service truck can look small next to a mixer and still be over the line. Check the GVWR on the door-jamb label before you assume it's out.

## How often: twice a year (for now)

CARB is clear that ["almost all vehicles are subject to semi-annual testing"](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements). Construction trucks aren't one of the exceptions. The annual schedule only applies to qualifying agricultural vehicles and recreational motorhomes.

For California-registered trucks, the deadlines are based on [the DMV registration expiration date, and every six months after that](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-requirements-vehicles-subject-semi-annual-compliance). Trucks with out-of-state or DMV-exempt plates follow a schedule based on the last digit of the VIN.

Two dates to put on the shop calendar:

- **The 90-day window.** You can submit a passing result [up to 90 days before the deadline](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements). Use it. Testing early in the window means a surprise check engine light doesn't turn into a parked truck. Our guide on [calculating the 90-day window](https://norcalcarbmobile.com/clean-truck-check-blog/how-to-calculate-90-days-for-carb-testing-compliance) shows how.
- **October 2027.** Starting then, CARB moves [OBD-equipped vehicles to four tests per year](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements). Any mixer or dump with a 2013 or newer diesel engine is an OBD truck, so plan for more test days.

## OBD or OVI: it depends on the engine

Your fleet probably needs both test types. The test is set by the engine model year, not the truck's job:

- **OBD test:** 2013 and newer diesel engines, and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines. We plug into the truck's on-board diagnostics. See [our OBD test page](https://norcalcarbmobile.com/carb-obd-test).
- **Smoke opacity + visual inspection (OVI):** 2012 and older diesel engines. CARB also requires this test for [off-road engines installed in on-road trucks](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements), no matter the year. That can include some specialty units. See [our OVI test page](https://norcalcarbmobile.com/carb-ovi-test).

If you're not sure, check the engine emissions control label, or send us the VINs and we'll sort it out before we drive over. For more on the difference, read [OBD vs OVI for fleets](https://norcalcarbmobile.com/blog/obd-vs-ovi-clean-truck-check-fleets).

## What happens if a truck falls behind

Construction schedules don't leave room for paperwork surprises. Here's what CARB says can happen:

- To be compliant, a truck needs a passing test, [its compliance fee paid, and no open enforcement](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-requirements-vehicles-subject-semi-annual-compliance). Non-compliance can lead to a DMV registration block.
- CARB and CHP run roadside emissions screening. A truck that gets a Notice to Submit to Testing needs [a passing test within 30 calendar days](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements).
- An OBD result of "Not Ready" or "Invalid Test" [isn't compliant](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements). The truck has to be retested. If a truck was just repaired or had its codes cleared, tell us. It may need some drive time before it will read "ready".

## How a yard visit works

1. **Send us the list.** Unit count, engine years or VINs, the yard address, and the deadlines you see in CTC-VIS.
2. **We match each truck to its test.** OBD or OVI is sorted before we arrive, not at the gate.
3. **We test at the yard.** Early starts are fine. The goal is to be done before the crews need the trucks.
4. **We submit the results.** CARB requires the [credentialed tester to submit results](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/clean-truck-check-emissions-compliance-testing-requirements), and you can see them in CTC-VIS.
