What an OVI Smoke-Opacity Test Is in Sacramento, and Who Needs It

If you are searching for an OVI test in Sacramento, you are usually looking for the Clean Truck Check smoke-opacity test for an older diesel truck, done at your yard. OVI is shop language. CARB’s official description is a smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the vehicle’s emissions-control equipment.

What an OVI test is

CARB’s emissions compliance testing requirements say the testing requirements depend on whether the vehicle or engine is equipped with OBD. Diesel non-OBD vehicles (2012 and older diesel engines) are required to complete a smoke opacity test and a visual inspection of the vehicle’s emissions-control equipment, referred to in the program as the Vehicle Emissions Control Equipment Inspection.

CARB’s FAQ says that when performing emissions compliance tests on non-OBD vehicles, a credentialed tester must use an SAE J1667-compatible smoke meter to conduct the smoke opacity test. That is the snap-acceleration smoke test procedure for heavy-duty vehicles. Our pricing page and OVI service page describe the mobile test the same way: SAE J1667 smoke opacity, at your location.

This is not a light-duty Smog Check. It is the heavy-duty Clean Truck Check path for engines that are not tested by an OBD scan. For the side-by-side fleet comparison, see OBD vs OVI for fleets.

The credentialed tester sends the result to CARB. You do not upload the test yourself. CARB, not the tester, decides compliance.

Which trucks need an OVI test

Use the engine model year on the emissions control label (ECL) on the engine, not the truck year on the door. CARB notes that most vehicles have an engine model year one year older than the vehicle model year, and tells owners to check the label so the correct test is performed.

Per the same testing-requirements page:

  • Diesel engines from 2012 and older: smoke opacity test plus the visual inspection. This is the Mobile OVI path.
  • Alternative-fuel engines from 2017 and older: visual inspection only. They are not subject to the smoke opacity test.
  • Off-road engines in on-road heavy-duty vehicles (over 14,000 pounds GVWR) that operate on public roads: opacity and visual inspection regardless of the engine model year, because there are currently no OBD requirements for off-road engines.
  • Diesel engines from 2013 and newer, and alternative-fuel engines from 2018 and newer: an OBD scan of the engine’s OBD data with a CARB-certified OBD testing device, not an OVI. See our OBD test.

If you are not sure, call (916) 890-4427 with the engine year from the label before we schedule the visit. We will confirm whether the truck needs a Mobile OVI or an OBD scan.

Clean Truck Check applies to nearly all diesel and alternative-fuel heavy-duty vehicles over 14,000 pounds GVWR that operate on California public roads, including many vehicles registered outside California. For trucks that are not in the program, see which trucks don’t need Clean Truck Check.

What happens at a Sacramento yard visit

We come to your yard, shop, or jobsite in the Sacramento area, including Elk Grove, Rancho Cordova, Folsom, Citrus Heights, North Highlands, Roseville, Rocklin, Davis, and Woodland, as listed on our Sacramento page. You do not have to drive the truck to a shop for this test.

On a Sacramento yard visit, we set up a river-city opacity yard-slip: the SAE J1667 snap-acceleration smoke test and the visual inspection of the emissions-control equipment.

A typical visit looks like this:

  1. We confirm the vehicle and the engine year on the emissions control label, so the correct test is performed.
  2. For a diesel OVI, the tester runs the SAE J1667 snap-acceleration smoke opacity test with an SAE J1667-compatible smoke meter, then completes the visual inspection of the emissions-control equipment. If the engine is alternative-fuel and only the visual inspection is required, that is the test that is done.
  3. The tester submits the result to CARB. NorCal CARB Mobile submits the result the same day as the visit. Same-day work covers the test and the submission only.

If the truck does not pass, CARB says the owner should complete any needed repairs and have the vehicle tested again and pass before the compliance deadline.

If you also have a due date or a DMV hold

A passing OVI is only one part of Clean Truck Check. The vehicle also has to be reported in CTC-VIS, and the annual compliance fee has to be paid. Your due date is not a single statewide calendar. For California-registered trucks, CARB sets the deadlines from the DMV registration expiration month and every six months thereafter. See when is my Clean Truck Check due?.

Frequently asked questions

Q: What is an OVI test?
A: OVI is shop language for the Clean Truck Check opacity and visual inspection. CARB requires a smoke opacity test and a visual inspection of the emissions-control equipment for diesel non-OBD vehicles. A credentialed tester must use an SAE J1667-compatible smoke meter for the opacity test.

Q: Which trucks need an OVI test?
A: Use the engine model year on the emissions control label, not the truck year on the door. Diesel engines from 2012 and older need the smoke opacity test plus the visual inspection. Alternative-fuel engines from 2017 and older need the visual inspection only, not the opacity test. An off-road engine in an on-road heavy-duty vehicle needs the opacity and visual inspection regardless of engine year. Diesel engines from 2013 and newer, and alternative-fuel engines from 2018 and newer, need an OBD scan instead.

Q: What happens during a Sacramento yard visit?
A: A CARB-credentialed tester comes to your yard or shop in the Sacramento area, confirms the engine year on the emissions control label, completes the required smoke opacity and visual inspection steps, using an SAE J1667-compatible smoke meter when an opacity test is required, and submits the result to CARB. You do not upload the test yourself. NorCal CARB Mobile submits the result the same day as the visit.

Q: How much does a mobile OVI test cost in Sacramento?

Q: Is an OVI the same as a light-duty smog check?
A: No. A Clean Truck Check OVI is a heavy-duty smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions-control equipment, performed by a CARB-credentialed tester. It is not a light-duty Smog Check.

Q: What if I am not sure which test my truck needs?
