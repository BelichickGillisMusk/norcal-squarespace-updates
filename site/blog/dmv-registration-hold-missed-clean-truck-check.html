DMV Registration Hold After a Missed Clean Truck Check? How to Catch Up and Avoid Penalties

Published November 14, 2026 · Vin CA Diesel

A Clean Truck Check deadline came and went, and now California DMV will not finish the registration. That is a common, fixable problem. The California Air Resources Board (CARB) and California DMV both describe how the hold works, what has to be completed, and how the hold comes off.

This page walks through a DMV registration hold for a missed or overdue Clean Truck Check test, how to catch up, and what official pages say — and do not say — about penalties. What a CA DMV Clean Truck Check (SB 210) registration hold is

CARB’s CA DMV Registration Hold (SB 210) fact sheet says Clean Truck Check applies to nearly all diesel and alternative-fuel heavy-duty vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 14,000 pounds that operate on California public roads and highways, even if they are not registered in California. Subject vehicles must be reported in the Clean Truck Check Vehicle Inspection System (CTC-VIS), pay the annual compliance fee, and complete emissions testing.

That same fact sheet states that CA DMV registration holds are automatically placed on vehicles that are not compliant with Clean Truck Check requirements. CARB’s agricultural-vehicle fact sheet uses the same point: DMV automatically places registration holds on vehicles not compliant with Clean Truck Check.

California DMV’s own procedures manual says DMV confirms compliance electronically, as identified by CARB, and will deny registration renewal or transfer of non-compliant heavy-duty vehicles over 14,000 pounds GVWR (Vehicle Code sections 4000.15, 4156.5, and 9257.5). In DMV’s industry system the flag can appear as “Q212 WARNING CARB NON-COMPLIANT (HDIM).”

CARB also says registration of a vehicle cannot be completed if a compliance requirement has not been met before newly registering the vehicle or before the registration expiration date. This type of hold prevents most DMV transactions.

A passing test by itself is not the whole program. See what Clean Truck Check is if you need the broader overview.

What “compliant” actually means

CARB’s overview fact sheet says a vehicle owner has to meet every compliance deadline and do all of the following:

  1. Report the vehicle in CTC-VIS.
  2. Pay the vehicle’s annual compliance fee.
  3. Submit a passing Clean Truck Check emissions test.
  4. Make sure the vehicle has no outstanding enforcement violations.

If any one of those is missing, the vehicle is not compliant, even if it already passed a test.

The annual compliance fee is CARB’s fee, not a tester’s price. CARB’s FAQ says the fee started at $30 per vehicle and adjusts with the California Consumer Price Index. CARB’s overview gives $31.18 as the example for 2025. Check CTC-VIS for the amount due on your vehicle this year. Report and pay through CTC-VIS.

Why a missed test often shows up around November and December

Almost all subject vehicles are on a semi-annual schedule — every six months. Agricultural vehicles that meet CARB’s definition, and California-registered motorhomes used for recreational use or emergency occupancy, test once per year. Commercial-use motorhomes follow the semi-annual table.

For California-registered vehicles, CARB says compliance deadlines are based on the DMV registration expiration date and every six months thereafter. Table I on that page pairs each registration-expiration month with two deadline months six months apart. In practical terms:

  • If the California registration expires in May, the two deadline months are May and November.
  • If it expires in June, the two deadline months are June and December.
  • If it expires in November or December, those months are themselves a deadline, and the other deadline falls six months later.

That is why a “second test” can come due in November or December even when the registration month was in the spring. CARB’s FAQ adds that the annual compliance fee is due on the first compliance deadline of the compliance year. Always use the dates in your own CTC-VIS account. CARB repeats that the deadlines shown there are what mark compliance.

You may submit a passing test up to 90 days before a compliance deadline. See our 90-day calculation guide and our 2026 testing-deadlines post.

Beginning October 2027, OBD-equipped vehicles will be required to test four times per year. Non-commercial motorhomes and agricultural vehicles, as defined in the regulation, remain annual.

How to catch up on a missed or overdue test

Work the items in order. Skipping the fee or the reporting step will not clear a hold, even after a passing test.

1. Log in to CTC-VIS and read the vehicle. See whether it is reported, whether the annual fee is paid, whether a passing test is on file, and whether any enforcement item is open. CARB’s monthly account summary lists vehicles with a deadline in the next 90 days and any open enforcement actions.

2. Report the vehicle if it is not already in the account. Every subject vehicle has to be reported in CTC-VIS.

3. Pay the annual compliance fee if it is unpaid. CARB says card payments can take 1–2 business days to process and TeleCheck (eCheck) payments can take up to 7 business days. Weekends and holidays do not count as business days.

4. Get a passing test from a CARB-credentialed tester. The test depends on the engine year on the emissions control label, not a guess from the door sticker. Per CARB’s testing requirements:

  • OBD scan for 2013 and newer diesel engines and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines. See our OBD test.
  • Smoke opacity plus visual inspection (the test shops call Mobile OVI) for 2012 and older diesel engines. Alternative-fuel engines from 2017 and older need the visual inspection only, not the opacity test. See our OVI test.

The credentialed tester sends the result to CARB. You do not upload the test yourself.

If the truck was just repaired, do not rush an OBD test that will not count. CARB says an OBD result of “Not Ready,” “Vehicle Not Applicable For OBD Testing,” or “Invalid Test” is not compliant and the vehicle has to be retested.

5. Download the compliance certificate from CTC-VIS. Once payment has processed and the other requirements have been met, the certificate is available under Vehicles on your My Entity page. CARB says you should print it or store it digitally for your own records. You do not need to show or send that certificate to DMV.

6. Wait for DMV to update, then contact DMV yourself. See the timing section below. DMV will not automatically send registration tags when the hold is removed. CARB recommends going in person to a DMV field office if possible, and also points owners to DMV’s Virtual Office. If DMV still shows the vehicle as noncompliant after CTC-VIS shows it compliant, CARB says to contact hdim@arb.ca.gov.

A separate clock applies if you received a Notice to Submit to Testing (NST). Even if the vehicle already passed its regular test, a passing Clean Truck Check test must be submitted within 30 calendar days of receipt. Follow the letter.

How long until DMV updates the hold

CARB publishes two related windows. Use the one that matches your situation, and build in processing time before you visit DMV.

  • If the vehicle met all compliance requirements before the due date (and no more than 90 days before the deadline), CARB’s FAQ says to allow 1–3 business days for that status to update with CA DMV. The SB 210 hold fact sheet likewise says the SB 210 hold “will be removed 1-3 business days after the compliance requirements for the upcoming deadline have been completed.”
  • Once a registration hold has already been placed, CARB’s FAQ and referee fact sheet both say to allow 3–5 business days from the date the certificate is downloaded for DMV to update.

CARB transmits a compliant VIN list to DMV on a nightly basis. Confirm the VIN shows as compliant in CTC-VIS before you check with DMV.

We do not issue the compliance certificate, and we cannot promise same-day DMV clearance. CARB issues compliance after the required items are complete.

How to avoid penalties — and what CARB actually says about fines

The surest way to stay off a hold is to finish the four compliance items before each deadline, using the 90-day testing window so there is time to repair a failed test and submit a passing result.

CARB’s semi-annual requirements page states that non-compliance may result in DMV registration blocks and other enforcement action. Subject vehicles must be reported in CTC-VIS and compliant before they may operate on California public roads and highways.

CARB’s pages about a regular missed compliance deadline describe enforcement action and DMV registration holds. They do not publish a separate dollar schedule that says “you missed your six-month test, so you owe $X.”

If you received a Notice to Submit to Testing and you do not have a test performed by that letter’s deadline, CARB says you could be liable for a Notice of Violation and assessed a penalty. CARB’s FAQ says owners who do not comply with the NST requirement may be subject to enforcement action, such as penalties and registration holds. The same FAQ states that violations of air pollution regulations are prosecuted under California Health and Safety Code section 42402, which identifies potential civil penalties of $1,000 to $10,000 per day per vehicle. That is CARB’s language about air-pollution-regulation violations in the NST answer — not a posted price list for a missed regular deadline.

Getting current, and staying on the CTC-VIS calendar, is how you keep those enforcement paths from opening.

Which test your truck needs

Use the engine year on the emissions control label. OBD versus OVI for fleets explains the split in shop language. In short, newer OBD-equipped engines get the scan; older diesel engines get the SAE J1667 smoke-opacity test plus the visual inspection.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Clean Truck Check DMV registration hold?

It is an automatic California DMV registration hold, labeled Clean Truck Check (SB 210), placed on a vehicle that is not compliant with Clean Truck Check. Most DMV transactions cannot be completed until the vehicle meets CARB’s requirements.

How do I get the hold removed?

Report the vehicle in CTC-VIS, pay the annual compliance fee, and submit a passing emissions test (and clear any outstanding enforcement). Then download the certificate from CTC-VIS and allow the published processing time before you contact DMV to finish registration.

Do I send my compliance certificate to DMV?

No. CARB says you do not need to show or send the certificate to CA DMV. After the hold is removed, you still have to contact DMV. Tags are not mailed automatically.

How long does DMV take to update?

CARB says allow 1–3 business days after you meet all requirements before the due date. Once a hold has already been placed, allow 3–5 business days from the date the certificate is downloaded. Weekends and holidays do not count.

What if I missed my second Clean Truck Check test this year?

Most trucks test every six months, with California deadlines tied to the registration-expiration month and the month six months later. Check CTC-VIS, complete any missing reporting or fee, and get a passing test submitted. November and December are common second-cycle months for vehicles whose registration expires in May or June.

Does a missed test come with a published fine amount?

CARB’s pages on a regular missed deadline describe enforcement action and DMV registration holds, not a posted per-test fine. If you miss an NST letter deadline, CARB says you could be assessed a penalty. CARB’s FAQ cites Health and Safety Code section 42402 potential civil penalties of $1,000 to $10,000 per day per vehicle for violations of air pollution regulations.

Who performs the test?

A CARB-credentialed tester, using a CARB-certified device. The tester submits the result.
