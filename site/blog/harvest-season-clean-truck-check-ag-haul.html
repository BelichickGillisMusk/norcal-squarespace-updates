Harvest season Clean Truck Check: ag can stay annual. Haul trucks cannot

Published September 17, 2026 · NorCal CARB Mobile · Woodland · Merced · Stockton · Porterville

Harvest does not pause for a station line. It also does not create a blanket exemption.

A qualifying agricultural vehicle under CARB’s Heavy-Duty Inspection and Maintenance rule can stay on an annual Clean Truck Check cycle. The diesel truck that hauls the crop on public roads is usually still on the semi-annual cycle — two tests in 2026, two tests through September 2027.

Annual is not exempt. Semi-annual is not optional. The source of truth for each VIN is the fleet’s CTC-VIS account, not a harvest calendar and not this page. Official cadence: CARB emissions compliance testing requirements.

Two clocks on one yard

The yard that mixes farm iron and highway haul is the failure mode.

  • Qualifying agricultural vehicles and qualifying California recreational / emergency motorhomes: 1 test per year now, and they stay annual after October 2027.
  • Most other heavy-duty diesels over 14,000 lb GVWR that operate on California public roads: 2 tests per year now through September 2027.
  • Beginning October 2027: OBD-equipped vehicles move to 4 tests per year. Opacity-path units (typically 2012-and-older diesel) stay semi-annual. Ag and those motorhomes stay annual.
  • Passing results may be submitted up to 90 days before the CTC-VIS deadline. Use that window so a fail can be repaired and retested before a DMV hold.

A grain hauler, livestock carrier, flatbed, or truck-tractor moving crop to the first point of processing is usually not the agricultural annual exception just because the load is agricultural. Confirm the unit in CTC-VIS.

What stops the truck is a CA DMV registration hold. A fine is a bill. A hold is a parked trailer that still costs insurance while the packing shed waits.

Testing notes from harvest corridors

  • Mixed lots are normal: one appointment can include an annual ag unit and a semi-annual haul truck. Do not put both on the same reminder cadence.
  • Engine year decides OBD vs OVI before dispatch. Unsure units get a VIN check on the call.
  • Do not clear codes or pull batteries before an OBD scan. “Not Ready” is a failed window.
  • Harvest timing is why we test at the yard. A station queue in Stockton or Merced is a missed load, not a compliance plan.
  • The 2025 ag explainer is still live and still useful for “does ag need a test at all.” This post is the 2026 cadence split. Read both: ag vehicles need testing.
