Harvest Season Clean Truck Check: Which Farm Trucks Stay Annual

Published October 6, 2026 · Vin CA Diesel

Post context: Location: Woodland, Merced, Stockton, Lodi · Industry: farm and harvest haul · Test: OBD or smoke opacity + visual (OVI) · Official CARB source: Clean Truck Check agricultural vehicle requirements

Harvest is the worst time to find out a truck's Clean Truck Check is out of date. The bins are full and the hullers are running. The tractor that sat all summer is suddenly doing three loads a day. And somebody in the office asks: "Wait, isn't ag annual?"

Sometimes, yes. But not every truck on a farm yard counts as an agricultural vehicle under the CARB rule. Here's the short version so you know which clock each unit is on before the busy weeks.

The default: twice a year

Under Clean Truck Check, CARB says that "almost all vehicles are subject to semi-annual testing". That covers heavy-duty vehicles over 14,000 lbs GVWR. For California-registered trucks, the deadlines are tied to the DMV registration expiration date and every six months after that.

There are two big exceptions that test once a year: agricultural vehicles that meet CARB's definition, and California-registered motorhomes used for recreation (CARB annual testing page).

What CARB counts as an agricultural vehicle

CARB defines an agricultural vehicle as "a vehicle or truck-tractor trailer combination owned or operated by a farming business and used exclusively in agricultural operations to transport agricultural products to the first point of processing".

Break that into three questions:

  1. Who runs it? It has to be owned or operated by a farming business.
  2. What is it used for? It has to be used only in agricultural operations. A truck that also does personal runs, or side work for someone else, doesn't fit.
  3. Where does the load go? To the first point of processing.

CARB's fact sheet lists what counts as a first point of processing. That includes packinghouses, slaughterhouses, cotton gins, nut hullers and shellers, dehydrators, lumber mills, feed and grain mills, and biomass facilities. In some cases it can even be in the field. It also lists what does not count: distribution centers, wholesale and retail sites, livestock auction houses, and later processing or canning locations (same CARB fact sheet).

So a farm-run truck hauling almonds from the orchard to the huller can stay annual. A truck hauling hulled nuts from the huller to a distribution center is past the first point of processing. That run doesn't meet the definition.

Two clocks on one yard

This is where harvest yards get tangled. Plenty of farm yards don't run just one kind of truck. A typical mix looks like this:

  • Farm-owned trucks doing field-to-packinghouse runs: these may qualify as agricultural vehicles (annual).
  • The same company's truck that also hauls for a neighbor or does a personal run: it's not "used exclusively in agricultural operations," so it's semi-annual.
  • A contract hauler's tractor that isn't owned or operated by the farming business: semi-annual under the default rule.
  • Anything running packinghouse-to-warehouse or cannery loads: past the first point of processing, so semi-annual.

The annual status isn't automatic, either. CARB says the owner claims it in the Clean Truck Check Vehicle Inspection System (CTC-VIS) with a checkbox and an attestation. If nobody checked that box, the system treats the truck like everything else.

What happens if a truck misses its deadline

CARB's ag fact sheet says the DMV automatically places a "Clean Truck Check (SB 210)" registration hold on non-compliant vehicles. In practice, you find out when the registration renewal stalls, right when you need the truck. To be compliant, the truck also needs its compliance fee paid and no open enforcement actions.

Roadside screening is also part of the program. If a truck gets a Notice to Submit to Testing, CARB requires a passing test within 30 calendar days.

One thing that won't change in 2027

Starting in October 2027, CARB moves OBD-equipped vehicles to four tests a year. CARB's testing page says that change does not apply to agricultural vehicles or non-commercial motorhomes. Those stay annual. That makes it worth getting the ag attestation right now, before the schedules split further.

Getting tested before harvest gets busy

You can submit a passing result up to 90 days before the deadline. That gives you room to test the whole yard in one slow morning instead of chasing deadlines in the middle of harvest. Our post on how to calculate the 90-day window walks through the math.

Which test does each truck need? It depends on the engine, not the truck's job:

  • OBD test: 2013 and newer diesel engines (and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines). See our OBD test page.
  • Smoke opacity + visual inspection (OVI): 2012 and older diesel engines, plus off-road engines installed in on-road trucks. See our OVI test page.

Check the engine emissions control label if you aren't sure. CARB says to go by the engine model year. Not sure your truck qualifies as ag at all? Read our guide: Is your truck an agricultural vehicle?
