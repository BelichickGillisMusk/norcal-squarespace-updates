Bay Area contractor trucks don't sit at one shop. They're at a staging lot in Hayward on Monday, a jobsite in Oakland on Tuesday, and parked behind a fence in Concord by Friday. Taking one across a bridge to a testing station can eat half a day, plus tolls.

Clean Truck Check still applies to all of them. NorCal CARB Mobile brings the test to wherever the trucks are.

Who needs Clean Truck Check

The program covers heavy-duty vehicles over 14,000 lbs GVWR operating in California. That includes privately owned, government and out-of-state trucks. For a typical contractor fleet that means dump trucks, mixers, tractors pulling equipment trailers, water trucks, and larger service-body trucks rated over 14,000 lbs.

Check the GVWR on the door-jamb label for anything borderline. Medium-duty work trucks are the ones people forget.

How often: twice a year

According to CARB, "almost all vehicles are subject to semi-annual testing". Jobsite trucks don't fall under the annual exceptions, which cover only qualifying agricultural vehicles and recreational motorhomes.

For California-registered trucks, the deadlines follow the DMV registration expiration date, then every six months. Out-of-state and DMV-exempt plates follow a schedule based on the last digit of the VIN. Your deadlines show in CTC-VIS, CARB's online system.

Two timing rules help a Bay Area fleet plan:

  • Test early. A passing result can be submitted up to 90 days before the deadline. Test the whole fleet when it's together, not one truck at a time as each deadline hits. Here's how to count the 90 days.
  • Plan for 2027. Starting October 2027, the four-times-a-year schedule applies to OBD-equipped on-road engines. On-road trucks with 2013 or newer off-road engines stay on smoke/OVI testing.

Which test each truck gets

The engine model year decides the test, according to CARB's testing requirements:

  • OBD test for 2013 and newer diesel engines and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines. More on our OBD test page.
  • Smoke opacity + visual inspection (OVI) for 2012 and older diesel engines, and for off-road engines in on-road trucks, whatever the year. More on our OVI test page.

Most mixed fleets need both. Send us the VINs or engine years and we'll match each truck to its test before we leave. For a side-by-side, see OBD vs OVI for fleets.

What Bay Area testing days look like

Bay Area visits come with their own quirks.

Split sites. One recent day had one truck in Martinez and one in Crockett, tested back to back because that's where the jobs were. We don't need the fleet in one place. We need an address and a gate time for each truck.

Weekend lots. Plenty of contractors want testing done when the trucks are parked, not working. A Saturday morning at a Redwood City yard is a normal appointment for us, not a special favor.

Bridges and gates. We plan the route around traffic and lot access, not the other way around. Tell us when the gate opens and when the trucks leave.

Retests. An OBD result of "Not Ready" or "Invalid Test" doesn't count as compliant. If a truck just had repairs or its codes cleared, tell us when you book. It may need drive time before the test.

Why staying current matters

CARB says compliance requires a passing test, the fee paid, and no open enforcement. Falling behind can lead to a DMV registration block. Roadside screening is part of the program too. A truck that gets a Notice to Submit to Testing needs a passing test within 30 calendar days.
