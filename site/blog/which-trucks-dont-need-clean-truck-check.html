Which Trucks Don't Need a Clean Truck Check?

Published October 30, 2026 · Vin CA Diesel

Post context: Informational guide to vehicles that are not subject to Clean Truck Check. Official CARB sources: Clean Truck Check FAQ and Vehicles Not Subject.

Most diesel and alternative-fuel trucks over 14,000 lbs GVWR that run on California public roads need Clean Truck Check: reporting in CTC-VIS, the annual fee, and a passing test. A smaller set of vehicles does not. CARB lists those exceptions on its FAQ and its vehicles not subject page. This post restates those lists so a fleet can check the door-jamb label and the plate before it books a test.

CARB reviews vehicles marked “not subject” in CTC-VIS. If the truck does not match a definition below, treat it as in the program.

Vehicles at or under 14,000 lbs GVWR

CARB’s FAQ says Clean Truck Check applies to heavy-duty vehicles with a manufacturer GVWR over 14,000 pounds. A truck at or under 14,000 lbs is not subject. Read the GVWR on the door-jamb label, not the registered weight or what the truck is carrying that day. Medium-duty service bodies sit on this line more often than people expect.

Authorized emergency vehicles

CARB’s vehicles not subject page points to California Vehicle Code section 165 or California Health and Safety Code section 1797.84. That is the legal definition of an authorized emergency vehicle, not a light bar on a contractor truck. CARB notes these units may still need a CTC-VIS record even when they are not subject to the test and fee.

Historical vehicles with permanent Historical Vehicle plates

The same CARB page limits this to vehicles issued a permanent Historical Vehicle License Plate under California Vehicle Code section 5004. A collector truck on regular commercial plates is still in Clean Truck Check. The historical designation has to show in CA DMV.

Military tactical vehicles

CARB cites Title 13, California Code of Regulations, section 1905. These are Department of Defense or U.S. military vehicles used in combat, combat support, combat service support, tactical or relief operations, or training for those operations. A contractor truck on a base job is not a tactical vehicle.

Vehicles under a CARB-issued experimental permit

CARB’s vehicles-not-subject page covers vehicles operating under a CARB-issued experimental permit authorized by California Health and Safety Code section 43014. CARB says those permits are rarely issued, and the owner has to produce the permit.

Zero-emission vehicles

Battery-electric and hydrogen fuel-cell trucks have no tailpipe emissions and are not subject. Natural gas, propane, ethanol, methanol, and other alternative fuels are not zero-emission. CARB’s FAQ is explicit that “alternative fuel” does not mean electricity or hydrogen. A diesel-to-electric conversion still needs referee verification before it can be treated as converted.

Out-of-state vehicles under a Governor’s Executive Order or Emergency Declaration

Out-of-state trucks that operate in California are in the program in the ordinary case. The exception on CARB’s vehicles-not-subject page is narrow: an out-of-state vehicle operating under a Governor’s Executive Order or Emergency Declaration, for 30 calendar days from the first day of operation under that order. Dispatch records have to show the emergency work. After 30 days, the truck is subject like any other out-of-state unit.

What this does not cover

Qualifying agricultural vehicles and California-registered motorhomes are still in Clean Truck Check; they test on a different schedule, they are not exempt. There is no low-use exemption. If you are unsure, use CARB’s two pages linked above, then check the GVWR label.
