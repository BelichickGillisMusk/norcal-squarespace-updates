Your DMV month sets the clock

Nobody runs a fleet to keep track of CARB paperwork. But the Clean Truck Check has dates, and missing one can hold up your registration. Here’s how the dates actually work, straight from CARB.

For California-registered trucks, your Clean Truck Check deadlines are based on your DMV registration expiration date, and every six months after that. Registration expires in March? Your testing deadlines land in March and September. CARB’s chart:

DMV registration expiresTesting and fee deadline months
Jan or JulJan and Jul
Feb or AugFeb and Aug
Mar or SepMar and Sep
Apr or OctApr and Oct
May or NovMay and Nov
Jun or DecJun and Dec

Out-of-state trucks and DMV exempt plates (state, local and federal government vehicles) go by the last digit of the VIN instead, also every six months. If your truck was ever registered with the California DMV, it stays on the DMV-month schedule even if it’s plated somewhere else now.

Either way, the dates in your CTC-VIS account (CARB’s Clean Truck Check reporting system) are the ones that count. If the DMV later changes your registration date, your Clean Truck Check deadline doesn’t move with it. You can also find your exact due date.

What's due at each deadline

  1. The truck is reported in CTC-VIS.
  2. A passing emissions test from a CARB credentialed tester. The tester sends it to CARB. You don’t upload anything.
  3. The annual compliance fee, paid at the first deadline of each compliance year. It’s $32.13 per vehicle now, and $33.12 for deadlines on or after January 1, 2027. CARB adjusts it every year with the California Consumer Price Index.
  4. No open enforcement violations on the truck.

How often you test

  • 2026: Almost every truck is on the twice-a-year schedule, so that’s one test every six months.
  • Starting October 2027: OBD-equipped trucks move to four tests a year.
  • Non-commercial motorhomes and agricultural vehicles stay on once a year.

Use the 90-day window

A passing test can be submitted up to 90 days before your deadline. CARB’s example: a truck due February 1 can submit as early as November 3. Testing early gives you time to fix a problem and retest before the deadline hits, instead of scrambling the week it’s due. CTC-VIS also emails a monthly summary of any trucks coming due in the next 90 days.

Which test each truck needs

It goes by the engine model year on the emission control label, not the year on the door. See OBD vs OVI for fleets.

  • OBD test ($75 per truck): 2013 and newer diesel engines, and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines.
  • OVI ($199): 2012 and older diesel engines get a smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions equipment.
  • Motorhome ($99/$229): Call and we’ll tell you which one fits.

If you’re already late

Get the truck tested as soon as you can, and check CTC-VIS for anything else that’s open, like an unpaid fee. CARB says non-compliance can lead to DMV registration blocks and other enforcement, and a late truck isn’t supposed to run on California roads until it’s compliant. Once everything shows compliant in CTC-VIS, CARB sends DMV its compliant list nightly. It can take a few business days for a registration hold to clear.

Got a Notice to Submit to Testing in the mail? That one has its own clock: a passing test is due to CARB within 30 calendar days.
