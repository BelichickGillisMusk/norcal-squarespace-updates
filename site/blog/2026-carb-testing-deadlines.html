2026 CARB Testing Deadlines for Fleet Operators

Published June 28, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026 · NorCal CARB Mobile

If you operate heavy-duty diesel or alternative-fuel vehicles in California, your 2026 Clean Truck Check deadlines are the dates in your CTC-VIS account. CARB required a passing emissions test for compliance deadlines on or after January 1, 2025, after emissions testing requirements took effect on October 1, 2024. A vehicle that is not compliant can receive an automatic California DMV registration hold.

Here is what official CARB pages say about how often you must test, which dates matter in 2026, and what happens if a vehicle is not compliant.

How often Clean Truck Check testing is required

CARB's emissions compliance testing requirements page says almost all vehicles are subject to semi-annual testing — every six months. Agricultural vehicles that meet CARB's definition, and California-registered motorhomes used for recreation or emergency occupancy, are required to test once per year. See also CARB's annual requirements for agricultural vehicles and California motorhomes.

Beginning October 2027, OBD-equipped vehicles will be required to undergo testing four times per year. That change does not apply to non-commercial motorhomes or agricultural vehicles as defined in the Heavy-Duty Inspection and Maintenance regulation; those stay on an annual schedule. Our four-times-per-year post quotes that official sentence.

Clean Truck Check applies to nearly all diesel and alternative-fuel heavy-duty vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 14,000 pounds that operate on California public roads and highways, even if they are not registered in California. That includes commercial vehicles, buses, California-registered motorhomes, and vehicles registered outside California (not including out-of-state motorhomes). Always refer to your CTC-VIS account for your due dates.

Key dates to use in 2026

Your own deadlines are the ones that count. For California-registered vehicles, CARB says compliance deadlines are based on the DMV registration expiration date and every six months thereafter. Confirm every date in CTC-VIS.

Official program dates, from CARB:

  • October 1, 2024: Emissions compliance testing requirements took effect.
  • Compliance deadlines on or after January 1, 2025: A passing compliance test is required as part of the demonstration.
  • Up to 90 calendar days before your deadline: You may submit a passing test early. See our 90-day calculation guide.
  • January 1, 2027: CARB's annual compliance fee rises to $33.12 for deadlines on and after this date. The current published fee is $32.13. This is CARB's fee, paid in CTC-VIS, not a tester price.
  • October 2027: OBD-equipped vehicles move to testing four times per year.

What happens if a vehicle is not compliant

CARB's CA DMV registration hold (SB 210) fact sheet says California DMV registration holds are automatically placed on vehicles that are not compliant with Clean Truck Check. To clear a hold, the vehicle must be reported in CTC-VIS, current on the annual compliance fee, and compliant with emissions testing requirements. The fact sheet says the SB 210 hold at DMV will be removed 1–3 business days after the compliance requirements for the upcoming deadline have been completed. You still have to contact DMV to finish the transaction. See our post on how to clear a DMV hold.

If CARB flags a vehicle as a potential high emitter, the owner may receive a Notice to Submit to Testing (NST). CARB says the owner then has 30 calendar days to submit a passing emissions compliance test. CARB's enforcement page says that if the test is not performed by the NST letter deadline, you could be liable for a Notice of Violation and assessed a penalty. CARB's FAQ also says vehicle owners that do not comply with the NST requirement may be subject to enforcement action, such as penalties and registration holds.

CARB coordinates enforcement with the California Highway Patrol. The FAQ states that CHP has the authority to conduct Clean Truck Check inspections, and that roadside inspections can include a check of reporting, fee payment, and emissions-test status.

How to stay ahead of your deadlines

The practical steps below follow the official windows. Always confirm your dates in CTC-VIS.

1. Know your CTC-VIS deadlines

Log in to CTC-VIS and note the testing deadline for every vehicle. If vehicles have different deadline months, keep a calendar of those official dates.

2. Use the 90-day testing window

CARB allows a passing test to be submitted up to 90 calendar days before the compliance deadline. Testing early leaves time for repairs and a retest if a vehicle does not pass on the first visit.

3. Use mobile testing to keep trucks on the route

A credentialed tester can come to your yard and submit the result. Published tester prices on our pricing page are HD-OBD $75 per truck, Mobile OVI $199 per truck, and motorhome $99 OBD / $229 OVI. What if you are already behind?

If you have missed a testing deadline, get a passing test submitted as soon as you can, then finish reporting, the annual fee, and any other open items in CTC-VIS so CARB can update the vehicle's status. Continuing to operate while non-compliant can lead to a registration hold and enforcement action.
