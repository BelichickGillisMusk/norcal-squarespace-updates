When Is My Clean Truck Check Due? Find Your Date from Your Registration Month

Published November 14, 2026 · Vin CA Diesel

Your Clean Truck Check due date is not one date for every truck in California. The California Air Resources Board (CARB) sets each vehicle’s compliance deadlines from a published rule, then shows those dates in the owner’s Clean Truck Check Vehicle Inspection System (CTC-VIS) account. For a California-registered truck, that rule starts with the DMV registration expiration month.

Your own CTC-VIS account always shows the official dates for each truck. If a missed deadline has already put a hold on the registration, see how to clear a Clean Truck Check DMV hold.

Not sure where a truck stands? Run the VIN through our Clean Truck Check VIN lookup.

Start in CTC-VIS, not with a statewide calendar

CARB’s emissions compliance testing requirements say to always refer to your CTC-VIS account for your due dates. The same page says the compliance deadlines will be available in the owner’s CTC-VIS account for each vehicle, and that a monthly account summary is emailed at the beginning of each month listing the number of vehicles with a compliance deadline in the next 90 days and the number of any open enforcement actions.

CARB’s FAQ also states that the deadlines shown in CTC-VIS are what are used to calculate your compliance status. If the table below and the portal disagree, use the portal.

How California-registered due dates are set

CARB’s semi-annual compliance guidance says compliance deadlines for California-registered vehicles are based on their DMV registration expiration dates and every six months thereafter. That rule also applies to vehicles historically registered with CA DMV, even if they are now registered outside of California.

CARB’s Table I lists the two testing months for each registration-expiration month. In that table, one of the two months is bold. After the official tables, CARB states that bolded months indicate the month the compliance fee will be due. The annual fee rule is described in the fee section below. Confirm both the test months and the fee date in CTC-VIS.

Example: if the California registration expires in May, the two deadline months are May and November, and May is the fee month in CARB’s table. If it expires in November, the two deadline months are still May and November, and November is the fee month in CARB’s table.

For a California-registered truck, treat the two windows as a reg-month due-pair chalkline: the DMV registration expiration month and the month six months later.

Special-plated vehicles that still follow the registration-expiration schedule, such as SE plates, are described in CARB’s FAQ: they follow a compliance deadline schedule according to the DMV registration expiration date, and CARB says to contact DMV for the official expiration date.

Out-of-state and DMV-exempt vehicles

If the vehicle was never historically registered with CA DMV, CARB’s semi-annual guidance uses a different table. Compliance deadlines for non-California-registered vehicles, DMV-exempt plated vehicles (state and local government), and federal government vehicles are based on the last number of the VIN and then recur every six months. Vehicles historically registered with CA DMV stay on the registration-month table above, even if they are now registered outside California.

How often you have to test in 2026, and what changes beginning October 2027

CARB’s testing-requirements page says almost all vehicles are subject to semi-annual testing, meaning every six months. Agricultural vehicles that meet CARB’s definition, and California-registered motorhomes for recreational use or emergency occupancy, are required to undergo testing once per year. See CARB’s agricultural vehicles and California motorhomes annual requirements. Motorhomes not used for recreational use or emergency occupancy, such as commercial-use motorhomes, follow the semi-annual requirements. Our motorhome testing page covers the mobile test itself.

The semi-annual rule is the current schedule for 2026. Use the months in the tables above and the dates in CTC-VIS.

Beginning October 2027, CARB says on-board diagnostics (OBD) equipped vehicles will be required to undergo testing four times per year. That change does not apply to non-commercial motorhomes or agricultural vehicles as defined in the Heavy-Duty Inspection and Maintenance regulation; they remain on an annual testing schedule. Check CTC-VIS for the exact dates as CARB posts them. See also our existing post, is Clean Truck Check testing four times this year?.

How early you can test

Passing Clean Truck Check test results may be submitted up to 90 days before a compliance deadline. CARB’s semi-annual guidance gives this example: a vehicle with a compliance deadline of February 1, 2025 may submit a passing emissions test as early as November 3, 2024.

A test submitted earlier than that 90-day window will not count for that deadline. The window is counted from the due date in CTC-VIS, not from a three-calendar-month guess. See how to calculate 90 days for CARB testing compliance.

Notice to Submit to Testing (NST) is a separate clock

A regular CTC-VIS deadline is not the only date that can require a test. Clean Truck Check also uses roadside emissions monitoring. CARB’s overview fact sheet and testing requirements say that a vehicle flagged as a potential high emitter will receive a Notice to Submit to Testing (NST). Upon receipt of an NST, a passing Clean Truck Check emissions compliance test must be submitted to CARB within 30 calendar days of receipt, even if the vehicle already passed its regular test.

Follow the instructions in the letter. CARB’s NST enforcement page says that if you do not have a test performed by the NST letter deadline, you could be liable for a Notice of Violation and assessed a penalty. Once CARB determines the vehicle has a passing emissions test, CARB says there is nothing more you will need to do in response to the NST.

If the missed item is already a DMV registration hold, use the DMV-hold walkthrough.

The annual compliance fee, including the 2027 update

Reporting, the annual compliance fee, and a passing test are separate requirements. CARB’s FAQ says the annual compliance fee is due on the first compliance deadline of the compliance year. Pay it in CTC-VIS. It is not NorCal CARB Mobile’s test price.

CARB’s Annual Vehicle Compliance Fee update 2027 describes the current fee as $32.13 and says the fee will be increased to $33.12 for the 2027 annual fee paid to satisfy requirements for vehicle compliance deadlines on and after January 1, 2027. CARB also says these fee updates are required to be published each year on CARB’s website by July 1, with the change taking effect the following year.

Which test your truck needs, and what it costs

The test depends on the engine year on the emissions control label, not a guess from the door sticker. Per CARB’s testing requirements:

  • OBD scan for 2013 and newer diesel engines and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines. See our OBD test.
  • Smoke opacity plus visual inspection (the test shops call Mobile OVI) for 2012 and older diesel engines. Alternative-fuel engines from 2017 and older need the visual inspection only, not the opacity test. An off-road engine in an on-road heavy-duty vehicle needs the opacity and visual inspection regardless of engine year. See our OVI test and the Sacramento answer post, OVI smoke-opacity test in Sacramento.

OBD versus OVI for fleets is the side-by-side comparison. The credentialed tester sends the result to CARB. You do not upload the test yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Q: When is my Clean Truck Check due?
A: Look in your CTC-VIS account. CARB says the compliance deadlines shown there are the dates used to calculate your compliance status. For most California-registered trucks, those dates fall in the DMV registration expiration month and the month six months later.

Q: How is the due date set for a California-registered truck?
A: CARB’s semi-annual guidance says compliance deadlines for California-registered vehicles are based on their DMV registration expiration dates and every six months thereafter. The same rule applies to vehicles that were historically registered with CA DMV, even if they are now registered outside California. Always confirm the dates in CTC-VIS.

Q: How often do I have to test in 2026, and what changes in 2027?
A: Almost all subject vehicles are on a semi-annual schedule, meaning every six months, through 2026. Agricultural vehicles that meet CARB’s definition, and California-registered motorhomes for recreational use or emergency occupancy, test once per year. Check CTC-VIS for the exact dates as CARB posts them.

Q: What is a Notice to Submit to Testing (NST)?

Follow the instructions in the letter.
