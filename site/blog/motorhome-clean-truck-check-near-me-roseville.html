Does a motorhome need Clean Truck Check?

Often, yes. CARB says Clean Truck Check applies to "nearly all diesel and alternative fuel heavy-duty vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 14,000 pounds" that drive on California roads. CARB's list specifically includes personal vehicles and California-registered motorhomes.

So a big diesel pusher registered in California and rated over 14,000 lbs GVWR is usually in. Check the GVWR on the manufacturer's label before you assume either way.

What about rigs from other states? CARB's list covers vehicles registered outside California, but it says "not including motorhomes". CARB's FAQ also lists out-of-state motorhomes as not subject to the program.

Personal use or commercial use: it changes the schedule

This is the big question for show-circuit owners.

  • Personal (recreational) use: California-registered motorhomes used for recreation or emergency occupancy test once per year.
  • Commercial use: CARB says motorhomes used commercially follow the semi-annual (twice a year) schedule like most trucks.

Your schedule depends on how the motorhome is used and registered, so check how it shows in CTC-VIS, CARB's online system. If you aren't sure which side you fall on, CARB's pages above are the official answer.

One more date to know: starting October 2027, CARB moves OBD-equipped vehicles to four tests a year. That change does not apply to non-commercial motorhomes. Those stay annual.

For a deeper walkthrough, see our complete guide to motorhome CARB testing and our post on requirements for personal, non-commercial motorhomes.

OBD or OVI: which test your motorhome gets

The engine model year decides the test, per CARB's testing requirements:

  • OBD test: 2013 and newer diesel engines (2018 and newer for alternative fuel). We connect to the on-board diagnostics port.
  • Smoke opacity + visual inspection (OVI): 2012 and older diesel engines. We run a smoke test and check the emissions control equipment.

The engine year can be different from the coach year, so check the engine emissions control label. If you can't find it, send us the VIN and we'll help sort it out.

A tip for OBD rigs: if the motorhome just had engine work or the codes were cleared, drive it a bit before the test. CARB counts an OBD result of "Not Ready" as not compliant, and that means a retest.

We test at your driveway, not the show grounds

We come to where the motorhome is parked: your driveway, storage lot or RV pad. Book a few days before you leave for a show weekend so the result is in before you pull out. You can submit a passing test up to 90 days before your deadline, so there's no reason to cut it close.

Our credentialed tester submits the result to CARB. CARB requires the tester to submit it, and you can check it in CTC-VIS afterward.
