How to Clear a DMV Registration Hold from Clean Truck Check Fast

Published November 14, 2026 · Vin CA Diesel

California DMV will not finish the registration, and the reason is a Clean Truck Check gap. That is a common, fixable problem. The California Air Resources Board (CARB) and California DMV both describe how the hold works, what has to be completed, and how the hold comes off.

The fastest path is to finish the missing items in the right order. NorCal CARB Mobile can do the emissions test at your yard and submit the result to CARB the same day. CARB decides compliance. You still complete the reporting, fee, and DMV follow-up steps below. We do not control how quickly DMV releases the hold.

Not sure where your truck stands? Run your VIN through our Clean Truck Check VIN lookup.

What a CA DMV Clean Truck Check (SB 210) registration hold is

CARB’s CA DMV Registration Hold (SB 210) fact sheet says Clean Truck Check applies to nearly all diesel and alternative-fuel heavy-duty vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 14,000 pounds that operate on California public roads and highways, even if they are not registered in California. Subject vehicles must be reported in the Clean Truck Check Vehicle Inspection System (CTC-VIS), pay the annual compliance fee, and complete emissions testing.

That same fact sheet states that CA DMV registration holds are automatically placed on vehicles that are not compliant with Clean Truck Check requirements. CARB’s agricultural-vehicle fact sheet uses the same point: DMV automatically places registration holds on vehicles not compliant with Clean Truck Check.

California DMV’s own procedures manual says DMV confirms compliance electronically, as identified by CARB, and will deny registration renewal or transfer of non-compliant heavy-duty vehicles over 14,000 pounds GVWR (Vehicle Code sections 4000.15, 4156.5, and 9257.5). In DMV’s industry system the flag can appear as “Q212 WARNING CARB NON-COMPLIANT (HDIM).”

CARB also says registration of a vehicle cannot be completed if a compliance requirement has not been met before newly registering the vehicle or before the registration expiration date. This type of hold will prevent most transactions at DMV.

A passing test by itself is not the whole program. See what Clean Truck Check is if you need the broader overview.

What has to be complete before the hold comes off

CARB’s overview fact sheet says a vehicle owner has to meet every compliance deadline and do all of the following:

  1. Report the vehicle in CTC-VIS.
  2. Pay the vehicle’s annual compliance fee.
  3. Submit a passing Clean Truck Check emissions test.
  4. Make sure the vehicle has no outstanding enforcement violations.

If any one of those is missing, the vehicle is not compliant, even if it already passed a test. To remove an SB 210 hold, the vehicle needs to be reported in CTC-VIS, compliant with the annual fee, and compliant with the emissions testing requirements.

The annual compliance fee is CARB’s fee, not a tester’s price. CARB’s FAQ says the fee started at $30 per vehicle and adjusts with the California Consumer Price Index. CARB’s overview gives $31.18 as the example for 2025. Check CTC-VIS for the amount due on your vehicle this year. Report and pay through CTC-VIS.

How to clear it fast

Work the items in order. Skipping the fee or the reporting step will not clear a hold, even after a passing test submitted the same day.

1. Log in to CTC-VIS and read the vehicle. See whether it is reported, whether the annual fee is paid, whether a passing test is on file, and whether any enforcement item is open. CARB’s monthly account summary lists vehicles with a deadline in the next 90 days and any open enforcement actions.

2. Report the vehicle if it is not already in the account. Every subject vehicle has to be reported in CTC-VIS.

3. Pay the annual compliance fee if it is unpaid. CARB says card payments can take 1–2 business days to process and TeleCheck (eCheck) payments can take up to 7 business days. Weekends and holidays do not count as business days. If the fee is still processing, start that step before or at the same time as the test so it is not the thing that keeps the hold in place.

4. Get a passing test from a CARB-credentialed tester. The test depends on the engine year on the emissions control label, not a guess from the door sticker. Per CARB’s testing requirements:

  • OBD scan for 2013 and newer diesel engines and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines. See our OBD test.
  • Smoke opacity plus visual inspection (the test shops call Mobile OVI) for 2012 and older diesel engines. Alternative-fuel engines from 2017 and older need the visual inspection only, not the opacity test. See our OVI test.

The credentialed tester sends the result to CARB. You do not upload the test yourself. NorCal CARB Mobile submits the result the same day as the yard visit.

If the truck was just repaired, do not rush an OBD test that will not count. CARB says an OBD result of “Not Ready,” “Vehicle Not Applicable For OBD Testing,” or “Invalid Test” is not compliant and the vehicle has to be retested.

5. Download the compliance certificate from CTC-VIS. Once payment has processed and the other requirements have been met, the certificate is available under Vehicles on your My Entity page. CARB says you can download it and print the certificate. You do not need to show or send that certificate to DMV.

6. Wait for DMV to update, then contact DMV yourself. See the timing section below. DMV will not automatically send registration tags when the hold is removed. CARB recommends going in person to a DMV field office if possible, and also points owners to DMV’s Virtual Office. If DMV still shows the vehicle as noncompliant after CTC-VIS shows it compliant, CARB says to contact hdim@arb.ca.gov.

A separate clock applies if you received a Notice to Submit to Testing (NST). Even if the vehicle already passed its regular test, a passing Clean Truck Check test must be submitted within 30 calendar days of receipt. Follow the letter.

Almost all subject vehicles are on a semi-annual schedule — every six months. For California-registered vehicles, CARB says compliance deadlines are based on the DMV registration expiration date and every six months thereafter. If the California registration expires in May, the two deadline months are May and November. If it expires in June, they are June and December. Always use the dates in your own CTC-VIS account. CARB repeats that the deadlines shown there are what mark compliance. You may submit a passing test up to 90 days before a compliance deadline. See our 90-day calculation guide and our 2026 testing-deadlines post.

How long DMV takes to update the hold

CARB publishes two related windows. Use the one that matches your situation, and build in payment-processing time before you visit DMV.

  • If the vehicle met all compliance requirements before the due date (and no more than 90 days before the deadline), CARB’s FAQ says to allow 1–3 business days for that status to update with CA DMV. The SB 210 hold fact sheet likewise says the SB 210 hold “will be removed 1-3 business days after the compliance requirements for the upcoming deadline have been completed.”
  • Once a registration hold has already been placed, CARB’s FAQ and referee fact sheet both say to allow 3–5 business days from the date the certificate is downloaded for DMV to update.

CARB transmits a compliant VIN list to DMV on a nightly basis. Confirm the VIN shows as compliant in CTC-VIS before you check with DMV. Weekends and holidays do not count as business days.

Frequently asked questions

Q: What is a Clean Truck Check DMV registration hold?
A: It is an automatic California DMV registration hold, labeled Clean Truck Check (SB 210), placed on a vehicle that is not compliant with Clean Truck Check. This type of hold prevents most transactions at DMV until the vehicle meets CARB’s requirements.

Q: How do I get the hold removed?
A: Report the vehicle in CTC-VIS, pay the annual compliance fee, and submit a passing emissions test (and clear any outstanding enforcement). Then download the certificate from CTC-VIS and allow the published processing time before you contact DMV to finish registration.

Q: How fast can the hold come off?
A: NorCal CARB Mobile can complete the test at your yard and submit the result to CARB the same day. CARB, not the tester, decides compliance and updates DMV. Once a registration hold has already been placed, CARB says to allow 3–5 business days from the date the certificate is downloaded for DMV to update. If all requirements were met before the due date, CARB says to allow 1–3 business days. Weekends and holidays do not count. Same-day work covers the test and the CARB submission only.

Q: Do I send my compliance certificate to DMV?
A: No. CARB says you do not need to show or send the certificate to CA DMV. After the hold is removed, you still have to contact DMV. Tags are not mailed automatically.

Q: What does a mobile Clean Truck Check test cost?

Q: Who performs the test?
A: A CARB-credentialed tester, using a CARB-certified device. The tester submits the result.
