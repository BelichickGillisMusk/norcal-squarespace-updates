Fleet Manager's Quarterly CARB Compliance Checklist

← Clean Truck Check Blog

If you manage a diesel fleet in Northern California — whether it's five trucks in Sacramento or fifty spread across the Central Valley — CARB's Clean Truck Check (CTC) program demands your attention every single quarter. Not because CARB tests every truck every three months (they don't, at least not yet), but because deadlines, fees, and vehicle statuses shift constantly across a mixed-age fleet, and one missed deadline can freeze a truck's registration and cost you $1,000–$10,000 per day in penalties.

This checklist is built for fleet managers who want a repeatable, 60-minute quarterly review that catches problems before they become citations. Run it in January, April, July, and October. We've structured it in five sections — CTC-VIS status, upcoming deadlines, fees, vehicle prep, and documentation — so nothing falls through the cracks.

Important: Fees, deadlines, and program details below are sourced from CARB's published guidance and current third-party compliance resources as of November 2026. Always confirm current requirements at cleantruckcheck.arb.ca.gov before acting.

Who Is Covered by CARB Clean Truck Check?

The rule applies to diesel and alternative-fuel trucks over 14,000 lbs GVWR. This includes California-registered vehicles and out-of-state trucks that operate in California. Gasoline vehicles are outside the rule; true zero-emission vehicles are exempt from reporting, fees, and testing. If you're unsure whether a specific truck is covered, check its GVWR on the registration and log in to CTC-VIS for its status.

Quarterly Compliance Checklist

Step 1 — Log In to CTC-VIS and Audit Every VIN

CARB's Clean Truck Check Vehicle Information System (CTC-VIS) is the single source of truth for your fleet's compliance status. Each quarter, log in and do a full audit:

CTC-VIS Audit Checklist

  • Verify every truck over 14,000 lbs GVWR is enrolled and reporting correctly.
  • Check the compliance status flag for each VIN — green (compliant), yellow (action needed), or red (non-compliant).
  • Look for any Notices to Submit to Testing (you have 30 days to pass once CARB issues one).
  • Add newly purchased or leased trucks; remove sold or retired units so you're not paying fees on vehicles you no longer own.
  • Confirm out-of-state trucks that regularly enter California are enrolled. Their deadlines are based on the last digit of the VIN, not DMV registration month.

Fleet managers with 20+ vehicles often find at least one enrollment discrepancy each quarter — a sold truck still showing, or a new acquisition that slipped through without enrollment. Catching this in CTC-VIS before CARB flags it is the difference between a quick fix and a formal enforcement notice.

Step 2 — Map Every Truck's Test Deadline for the Next 90 Days

This is the most time-sensitive part of the quarterly review. CARB's Clean Truck Check does not use a single fleet-wide deadline — each vehicle has its own testing window.

  • California-registered trucks: Deadlines are tied to the vehicle's DMV registration expiration month, then every six months after each passed test.
  • Out-of-state and DMV-exempt trucks: Deadlines are assigned based on the last digit of the VIN.
  • Key rule: A passing test submitted up to 90 days before the deadline still counts — meaning you can and should test early. Build that 90-day window into your scheduling calendar right now.

For the November quarterly review, that means you should be looking at deadlines through mid-February 2027 and scheduling tests now. NorCal CARB Mobile comes to your yard — Sacramento fleets can often be scheduled within 48 hours.

Deadline Tracking Checklist

  • Pull each VIN's next test due date from CTC-VIS.
  • Flag every truck due in the next 90 days as "schedule now."
  • For trucks with 2013+ diesel engines or 2018+ alternative-fuel engines: schedule an OBD test (CARB-certified scanner, ~$75 with NorCal CARB Mobile).
  • For trucks with pre-2013 diesel engines: schedule a smoke opacity (J1667) test plus equipment inspection (see mobile OVI testing near you).
  • Check if any trucks have a reduced-NOx engine certification — these may carry a multi-year testing exemption. Confirm status directly in CTC-VIS.

Step 3 — Confirm Annual Fee Payment for Every Enrolled Vehicle

The Clean Truck Check annual compliance fee is assessed per vehicle per year. Based on current compliance guides, the fee is approximately $32 per vehicle in 2026 (some sources cite $32.13; verify the exact current amount in your CTC-VIS account). The fee is expected to adjust slightly in 2027. A vehicle that passes its emissions test but has an unpaid fee is still non-compliant.

Fee Verification Checklist

  • Log in to CTC-VIS and confirm fee payment status for every enrolled vehicle.
  • Pay any outstanding fees immediately — do not wait for a DMV hold notice.
  • Update your accounting records for the new fee amounts effective January 1, 2027.
  • If you added trucks mid-year, confirm whether a pro-rated fee is owed for those units.

Step 4 — Run a Quick Pre-Test Vehicle Inspection

The most expensive compliance problem is scheduling a test, sending the truck, and watching it fail on something you could have spotted the week before. A simple pre-test walk-around saves time and re-test fees. Add this to your quarterly dispatch routine for every truck coming up on a 90-day window.

Pre-Test Vehicle Checklist

  • Check engine light: Any active emission-related fault codes cause an automatic OBD failure. Clear any active DTCs only after the underlying repair is complete, not before.
  • DEF system: Top off the DEF tank with high-quality fluid. Check for warning lights related to DEF quality, level, or injector function. Crystallized DEF or clogged injectors are a leading cause of fleet test failures.
  • DPF condition: Confirm the DPF has completed recent regeneration cycles. Trucks with lots of short-haul or high-idle time may need a forced regen before testing.
  • EGR and SCR: No bypasses, deletes, or aftermarket modifications to any emission control equipment. CARB considers tampered equipment an automatic failure and an enforcement violation.
  • Exhaust system: Look for visible leaks or soot buildup. For pre-2013 trucks, excessive visible smoke is a quick fail on opacity.
  • Warm the engine: OBD monitors need to be in a "ready" state. Drive the truck at highway speeds for 20–30 minutes before the test. Cold-engine OBD scans often return incomplete monitor readiness, which counts as a fail.

Step 5 — Audit Your Compliance Records

CARB can audit your fleet at any time. Keep records organized so you can respond quickly.

Records Checklist

  • Save all passing test certificates and confirmation emails from CARB — by VIN, indexed by date.
  • Keep repair invoices tied to any test failures — these demonstrate good-faith compliance efforts if you're ever audited.
  • Make sure every driver knows their truck's current CTC status and next test deadline.
  • If you use third-party compliance tracking software, verify it's pulling live data from CTC-VIS rather than relying on a stale export.

What's Coming in 2027: Quarterly OBD Testing

Starting October 2027, CARB is expected to move OBD-equipped vehicles to quarterly testing submissions — meaning most modern diesel trucks will need to report a passing OBD result four times per year rather than twice. Non-commercial motorhomes and certain agricultural vehicles are expected to remain on annual testing schedules. Confirm the specific implementation dates and vehicle categories at cleantruckcheck.arb.ca.gov as CARB finalizes the rollout.

If your fleet has 10 or more trucks, you should be planning now for the operational shift: quarterly testing for every OBD-eligible vehicle means your scheduling cadence roughly doubles. Mobile, on-yard testing at your facility is the most practical solution for most Northern California fleets — there's no driver downtime for a shop trip, and multiple trucks can be tested in a single visit.

What Happens if You Miss a Deadline?

Non-compliance triggers a cascade of consequences, each more expensive than the last:

  • DMV registration hold: CARB notifies DMV, and your vehicle's registration cannot be renewed until compliance is restored.
  • Port and railyard denial: Non-compliant trucks can be turned away at California ports and railyards — a potentially catastrophic disruption for logistics operations.
  • Notice to Submit to Testing: CARB issues a 30-day deadline to pass. Missing that triggers formal enforcement.
  • Civil penalties: Published enforcement guidance cites penalties of $1,000 to $10,000 per vehicle per day for unresolved non-compliance. For a fleet of 20 trucks, that exposure compounds fast.

The quarterly checklist exists to prevent ever getting to this point. A 60-minute review every three months is a small investment against five-figure daily fines.
