Why jobsite testing fits East Bay crews

Pipe, paving and utility jobs around Oakland and San Leandro run on dump trucks, vacuum trucks, flatbeds and support rigs. Most are diesel, and most are over 14,000 lbs gross vehicle weight rating. That puts them in CARB's Clean Truck Check (the Heavy-Duty Inspection and Maintenance program).

Pulling trucks off a job to drive them to a station costs drivers and trucks hours of work. So we bring the test to the trucks.

How a jobsite test works

We set up in the staging area or at your yard. 2013 and newer diesel engines get an OBD scan. 2012 and older diesel engines get a smoke opacity test plus a visual inspection of the emissions equipment. The tester sends the passing result to CARB. You don't upload anything. An Oakland or San Leandro visit can be a San Leandro yard-gate scan: we test at the gate or the jobsite and submit the result to CARB.

We come to your yard, so you don't burn $8 diesel.

Which test each truck needs

It goes by the engine model year on the emission control label, not the year on the door.

  • OBD test ($75 per truck): 2013 and newer diesel engines, and 2018 and newer alternative-fuel engines.
  • OVI ($199): 2012 and older diesel engines.
  • Motorhome ($99/$229): Call and we'll tell you which one fits.

Proof for your general contractor

A general contractor may ask every sub for proof of compliance before the first day on site. Your proof lives in CTC-VIS, CARB's Clean Truck Check reporting system. Once a truck is reported, the annual fee is paid and the test is in, its compliance certificate is in the Vehicles section of your CTC-VIS account.

The annual fee is $32.13 per vehicle now, and $33.12 for deadlines on or after January 1, 2027.

Test before the job starts

Most trucks test twice a year, every six months. You can submit a passing test up to 90 days before a deadline, so line up the trucks coming due before a long job starts. If a truck is non-compliant, CARB says it can face a DMV registration block and other enforcement.
